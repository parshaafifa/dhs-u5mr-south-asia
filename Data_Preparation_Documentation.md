# Data Preparation Documentation — Pooled DHS Under-Five Mortality Analysis

Countries: Bangladesh (2022), Nepal (2022), Pakistan (2017-18), India (2019-21)

This document records every substantive data-handling decision made while
preparing the pooled analytic sample, for inclusion in the methods section
and for reproducibility.

## 1. Outcome variable (`died_under5`)

A child is classified as:
- **1 (died)**: died before 60 completed months (`b5==0`, `b7<60`)
- **0 (survived)**: survived to at least 60 months, whether currently alive
  (`b5==1` and age at interview ≥60 months) or died at ≥60 months
  (`b5==0`, `b7≥60`)
- **Missing**: currently alive and under 60 months at the time of interview
  — a genuinely censored case, correctly excluded

**Correction applied during review:** an earlier draft additionally required
`age_at_interview ≥ 60` before a case could enter the analytic sample. This
incorrectly excluded fully-observed deaths that happened to occur in a
recent birth cohort (a child who died at 8 months but was born only 20
months before the survey was wrongly dropped). Removing this redundant
condition recovered thousands of real deaths across all four countries and
raised crude mortality estimates by 0.7–1.7 percentage points per country.
Outcome eligibility now depends only on `!is.na(died_under5)`.

## 2. India `b11` (preceding birth interval) special codes

27 India records carry `b11 == 997`. This is confirmed directly against the
DHS `.dta` file's own value label as `"inconsistent"` — not a valid birth
interval. These cases are excluded from `birth_interval_cat` via the
`b11 < 900` condition in the category logic, and correctly show as missing
rather than being miscoded into a real interval category.

## 3. Pakistan zero-weight observations (Azad Jammu & Kashmir / Gilgit-Baltistan)

9,058 Pakistan records (718 deaths, 8,340 survivors, concentrated in a
contiguous block of 103 PSUs, `v001` 471–580) have a sampling weight
(`v005`) of exactly 0.

This is a deliberate DHS design choice, confirmed via the DHS Program's
official user forum: the 2017-18 PDHS surveyed Azad Jammu & Kashmir (AJK)
and Gilgit-Baltistan (GB) but assigned them zero standard weight because
these regions are reported separately from Pakistan's national totals, for
political reasons specific to the survey round. The correct weight for
these two regions is a separate variable (`sv005`), normalized independently
within AJK/GB, which cannot be pooled with the rest of Pakistan or with the
other three countries in this study.

**Decision:** AJK and GB are excluded from the analytic sample via
`weight_raw > 0`. This is consistent with DHS's own convention for Pakistan
national estimates.

**Suggested methods-section language:**
> "Pakistan's analytic sample excludes Azad Jammu & Kashmir and
> Gilgit-Baltistan, consistent with DHS Program convention, as these
> regions carry a separate, non-comparable sampling weight (`sv005`) and
> are excluded from PDHS national totals."

## 4. Label conflicts across pooled countries (`v022`, `v023`, `v106`)

`dplyr::bind_rows()` raises warnings that value *labels* (not values) for
`v022`, `v023`, and `v106` conflict across the four country files, and
retains only the first-loaded country's label text. This affects display
labels only. Each country's `v106` was checked independently against its
own original file and confirmed to use the same 0/1/2/3 =
no-education/primary/secondary/higher scheme. All derived analysis
variables (`mat_edu`, `wealth`, `residence`, `child_sex`,
`birth_order_cat`, `birth_interval_cat`) are built from underlying numeric
codes via `case_when()`, not from label text, and are therefore unaffected.
